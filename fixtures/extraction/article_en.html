Rivers of the Northern Plains

By Ada Fernsby, published 3 November 2026

The northern plains are drained by a network of slow, meandering rivers whose courses shift with every spring flood. Hydrologists have mapped more than forty abandoned channels that once carried the main flow.

Farmers along the banks depend on the seasonal overflow, which deposits fine silt across the floodplain and renews the soil. In dry years the rivers shrink to chains of ponds, and irrigation cooperatives ration the remaining water.

Measuring change

Since 1998 a volunteer program has recorded water levels at seventeen gauging stations. The records show that peak discharge now arrives eleven days earlier than it did a generation ago.

The river remembers every dam we build, said one longtime observer.

Researchers caution that a single decade of data cannot separate long-term trends from natural variability.
